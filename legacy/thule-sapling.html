The product

Tracking down a squeak

Technical backpacks have a frame inside the back panel, usually stainless steel or aluminum, that keeps the lumbar support tensioned while allowing airflow. In one of our last sample rounds we heard a squeak from the bottom of the bag. We had two suspects:

  1. The plastic component where the back panel frame is inserted.
  2. The PU coating on the back of the fabric rubbing against the aluminum frame while the bag moves.

After extended wear testing we found the source: the PU coating rubbing against the aluminum frame. This was a last-minute problem, so I costed each fix and laid out the pros and cons.

Option 1

Anodize the frame and add nonwoven to the back of the panel

Pros
  • Adds strength to seams
  • Reduces sound
  • No additional materials for chemical testing
  • No visible change to the consumer
Cons
  • Adds $0.11 in material and labor
Option 2 · Recommended

Anodize the frame and reverse the panel so the PU side faces out

Pros
  • Reduces noise
  • No additional materials for chemical testing
  • Consumption and costing stay the same
Cons
  • The back side of the fabric is visible inside the storage area
