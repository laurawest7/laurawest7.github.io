Trusst Lingerie

Trusst support structure

I co-founded Trusst Lingerie to design better bras for fuller-busted women. Existing bras don't fully support them: underwires cause pain and too much weight goes to the shoulder straps. We built a support system that moves the weight of a larger bust onto the core of the body.

The product

The support structure is flexible, cantilevered and articulating. It is injection molded, then over-molded into a bra cup. The finished cups are assembled into the garment with specialty wicking fabrics to manage moisture.

Step 1 · Support structure design

The structure had to support the breast from underneath and carry the weight to the core of the body, while staying flexible and comfortable as the body moves. We settled on an articulated, cantilevered design and refined it by iterating quickly through 3D printing before investing in injection molding tooling.

We also tested several plastics, balancing flexibility against the heat resistance needed for washing and drying and overall durability. We chose a nylon.

Support structure

The design had to cover eight cup volumes with as few structures as possible to keep cost and complexity down. Four structure sizes cover all eight volumes, and I created the injection molding tooling for final production.

Seam allowances meant the structure needed adjustable length so it could be trimmed per bra size. The nylon supports have 1 cm of variance at each end. We worked with our cup factory on a custom die-cut tool to trim them precisely.

Molding the cups

We also developed a process with the cup factory to place the structure accurately inside the foam cups.

Step 3 · Cut and sew

Once the cups arrive at the sewing factory they are sewn into bras. The rigid structure inside makes sewing more physically demanding than a traditional underwire bra.

The lace edges also meant pattern layout and markers had to be chosen carefully, and production workers were trained to place patterns for minimum waste.
