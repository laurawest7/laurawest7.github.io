The suit

4 main components · 3 auxiliary

The V4 Safe Lift Suit has four main components and three small auxiliary components.

  1. Actuator and back panel

    The actuator lives inside the back panel, under a softgoods cover that protects it from the environment and conceals our IP. It holds a motor driven by proprietary software. The software locates the user in space using IMUs on the center back and behind each thigh, and adapts in real time to assist the lift.

  2. Shoulder straps

    The shoulder straps detach from the back panel so the suit can be adjusted to different user heights.

  3. Thigh wraps

    A wrap encircles each thigh and routes cording from the main actuator into an IMU unit at the center back of the thigh. The wraps come in multiple sizes and adjust with a lacing system.

Hardware on the force path

Both of these fasteners sit on the force path of the suit, so a failure here is a failure of the product.

Shoulder strap to battery pod buckle

The male buckles have a secondary locking nub that nestles into a divot in the female buckle. I tested riveted and sewn samples to confirm that this locking feature actually increases the breaking strength of the connection.

The locking nub broke off during testing. This was the common failure across all samples.

Steel to aluminum G-hooks

G-hooks that had been in the field for a long time were rusting. The V4 and V4A hooks are powder-coated steel and rust when exposed to moisture or laundering. We wanted to switch to aluminum, which does not rust, but we were concerned about the tensile strength of the nominated part.

Tensile testing confirmed the aluminum hook meets our spec. It is weaker than steel, but 400 N is still stronger than necessary.

Abrasion testing

EN-388 · Martindale

The cover fabric was wearing through in the field. I ran abrasion tests using an adapted version of EN-388 on a Martindale abrasion tester to find a more durable replacement. After testing and inspection, we moved to 840D Nylon Cordura.

The newest development wraps a composite material around the top of the device. Composites are generally more durable, but the choice of top, middle and bottom layers depends on the use case. I tested two varieties of X-Pac, a laminated composite with an internal Kevlar scrim.
